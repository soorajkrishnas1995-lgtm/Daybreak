Neuroscience · Alzheimer's

A hidden tipping point in brain immune cells may decide who gets Alzheimer's

Analyzing donated brain tissue from older adults -- including cognitively healthy centenarians -- researchers from VIB, KU Leuven and the UK Dementia Research Institute found a critical transition in microglia, the brain's immune cells, between an early amyloid-driven inflammatory state and a later, more damaging tau-linked state. Published in Nature Medicine, the findings suggest dementia risk may hinge less on how much Alzheimer's pathology someone has than on how their brain's immune cells respond to it -- pointing to a new drug target.
