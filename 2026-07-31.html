Iran War

US strike kills family on Qeshm Island; Iran retaliates against US bases in Kuwait, Jordan and Bahrain

CENTCOM carried out its largest strike wave yet on IRGC sites inside Iran, roughly double the size of the previous bombardment, hitting command centers, missile facilities and coastal defenses. One strike hit a residential building on Qeshm Island, killing a mother, father and their two-year-old child and injuring two more children. Iran's IRGC retaliated with strikes on US bases in Kuwait, Jordan and Bahrain that killed a worker, while Saudi Arabia joined US strikes on Iran-linked sites for the first time and is forming a 14-country Gulf naval coalition. The US Senate rejected a war-powers resolution to limit the strikes.

AI Security

Nvidia, Microsoft and SpaceX launch an 'Open Secure AI Alliance' after a major OpenAI-linked cyberattack

A roughly 40-member coalition that includes Microsoft, SpaceX, Palantir, IBM, Cisco, Cloudflare, CrowdStrike and Adobe will jointly build open-source defenses against AI-powered cyberattacks. The alliance was formed after an OpenAI model was reportedly used to breach Hugging Face's systems through a previously unknown zero-day vulnerability, raising fresh alarm across the industry about how quickly AI tools can be weaponized for offensive hacking. Member companies say they will share threat intelligence and jointly fund research into detecting and blocking AI-driven attacks before they can cause similar damage elsewhere.

Public Health

A record US cyclosporiasis outbreak tied to Mexican iceberg lettuce keeps growing rapidly across multiple states

CDC and FDA traceback investigations point to Taylor Farms de Mexico iceberg lettuce as the likely source of a parasitic outbreak that has now sickened nearly 2,000 people and hospitalized 98 of them across multiple US states. The cyclospora parasite causes prolonged, watery diarrhea and can take weeks to clear without proper antibiotic treatment. Major retailers have begun shunning produce sourced from central Mexico altogether rather than risk further contamination, even as health officials continue trying to pin down exactly where in the supply chain the parasite entered the lettuce.

US measles cases hit a 35-year high for a second record year

There have been 2,318 confirmed measles cases in the US as of July 23, already surpassing all of 2025's total and marking the worst count since elimination was declared in the year 2000, with 93% of this year's cases linked to identifiable outbreaks. Public health officials attribute the surge largely to declining childhood vaccination rates in several states.

CNN

Self-correcting silicon quantum chip cuts control errors by a factor of ten

HRL Laboratories demonstrated an 18-qubit chip that runs its own error correction through an on-board cryogenic controller, showing roughly fivefold error suppression as the number of qubits scales up, a meaningful step toward practical, fault-tolerant quantum computing systems. Researchers say the approach could eventually reduce the number of physical qubits needed to build a useful quantum computer.

Nature

NASA's Psyche spacecraft aces a Mars gravity-assist flyby en route to asteroid

The probe used Mars's gravity for a 1,000-mile-per-hour speed boost while imaging the planet's craters and polar ice cap and testing its onboard science instruments, ahead of its scheduled 2029 arrival at the metal-rich asteroid Psyche. NASA scientists said all instruments performed as expected during the flyby, clearing the mission for its next phase.

ScienceDaily

World's longest-running soil-warming experiment reveals a previously hidden carbon-release risk

After 37 years of continuous monitoring, researchers found that warming triggers microbial breakdown of previously 'protected' stable soil carbon, a feedback loop that most current climate models fail to capture and that could meaningfully accelerate future warming projections. The findings suggest current climate models may be underestimating how quickly soils could release stored carbon as temperatures rise.

ScienceDaily

Marine cloud brightening could destabilize the El Niño-La Niña climate cycle

Modeling shows that brightening clouds over the eastern Pacific Ocean to fight global warming could erase up to two-thirds of natural El Niño/La Niña variability, a side effect researchers say argues strongly against deploying the geoengineering technique in that particular region. The study adds to a growing body of research urging caution before any large-scale geoengineering deployment moves forward.
