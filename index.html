Human Origins

Modern humans carry DNA from two previously unknown extinct 'ghost' ancestor populations, study finds

A new technique called TRACE, which reconstructs ancestral relationships from present-day genomes without needing ancient fossil DNA, found that roughly 2% of the modern human genome comes from archaic hominins beyond the known Neanderthal and Denisovan contributions. One 'ghost' lineage that split off some 800,000 years ago interbred with humans in Africa before 50,000 years ago; a second, 1.8-million-year-old 'super-archaic' lineage passed DNA to modern humans indirectly through Denisovans. The archaic segments are concentrated in immunity and metabolism genes, researchers at UC Berkeley reported in the journal Science.

Also today

Blood pressure guidelines are 'missing half the equation,' nutrition researchers say

University of Vermont researchers argue that controlling blood pressure works far better when sodium reduction is paired with higher potassium intake, rather than sodium restriction alone. More than 1.28 billion adults worldwide have hypertension, and most people consume potassium well below optimal levels, the team reported in the American Journal of Clinical Nutrition.

ScienceDaily

Physicists build the world's first all-optical photonic time crystal, cutting photon losses

An international team created a material whose optical properties flip repeatedly on trillionths-of-a-second timescales using terahertz-frequency modulation, roughly halving photon losses. Researchers from Ecole Polytechnique, College de France and Germany's Helmholtz-Zentrum Dresden-Rossendorf say the breakthrough, published in Nature, could enable new tunable lasers and ultrafast computing and imaging.

ScienceDaily

Black holes may expel nearly as much matter as they swallow, study finds

Using the European Southern Observatory's Very Large Telescope, astronomers tracked black hole system Swift J1727.8-1613 through its 2023 eruption and found it kept expelling large amounts of gas via jets and winds even after dimming to about 1% of peak brightness -- challenging the 'bottomless pit' model of black holes.

ScienceDaily

Childhood BMI 'warning sign' for obesity may be based on a flawed assumption

A study of more than 2,400 US children found that while BMI rises around age six -- long treated as an early obesity warning sign -- a better fat-proxy measure, waist-to-height ratio, keeps falling at that age. Researchers say the BMI rise instead reflects muscle and lean-tissue growth, challenging a 42-year-old clinical assumption.
