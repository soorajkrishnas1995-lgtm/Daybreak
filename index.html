Choco Region · Colombia Earthquake

Magnitude 7.4 earthquake kills at least 250 in western Colombia as thousands remain missing

A magnitude 7.4 earthquake struck western Colombia's Choco region at 7:34am on Monday, killing at least 250 people and injuring hundreds across Cali, Pereira, Quibdo and nearby Risaralda, Caldas and Antioquia. More than 2,700 people remain missing on citizen-run tracking sites, and roughly 1,600 buildings have been damaged or destroyed. It is Colombia's strongest earthquake in two decades, and the Geological Service has recorded more than 70 aftershocks since the quake, complicating a rescue effort involving the military, police, engineers and search dogs. Officials have warned the confirmed death toll will likely keep rising as crews reach harder-hit areas.

Public Health · Ocean Heat

Marine heatwaves are emerging as a hidden and growing threat to human health

Researchers from Adelaide University and the University of Hong Kong warn that marine heatwaves should be treated as a public health concern in their own right, not just an environmental problem. Sustained periods of abnormally warm ocean water fuel stronger storms, trigger harmful algal blooms that threaten food safety, reduce seafood supplies and disrupt coastal livelihoods, while also driving anxiety, grief and depression in communities whose identity is tied to the ocean. The researchers point to a 2025 toxic algal bloom in South Australia that caused respiratory illness alongside psychological distress, and recommend governments plan for marine heatwaves the way they already plan for land-based ones.
