Nepal-China glacial flood disaster kills over 1,000 as Bihar and Uttar Pradesh go on high alert

Public Health · Cardiology

New 'BioTAK' blood test distinguishes broken-heart syndrome from real heart attacks with about 90% accuracy

Researchers at the University of Zurich and collaborators developed the BioTAK score, combining a patient's biological sex with blood biomarkers, to identify Takotsubo, or 'broken heart,' syndrome without invasive catheterisation. Validated in nearly 1,800 patients, the tool classified the condition with about 90% accuracy and revealed that Takotsubo runs on distinct stress and brain-heart pathways rather than the artery blockages behind typical heart attacks. The condition mostly affects postmenopausal women after emotional or physical stress, and earlier, non-invasive diagnosis could speed treatment decisions.
