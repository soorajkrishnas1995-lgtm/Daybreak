Nagoya, Japan · Oncology

An immune protein older than the human circulatory system could unlock far stronger cancer immunotherapy

Nagoya University researchers found that complement protein C3, when produced locally inside tumors rather than circulating in the bloodstream, blocks immune-suppressing cells from building up in the tumor microenvironment and gives immunotherapy a much better chance of working. Patients with higher tumor-tissue C3 levels responded to treatment about twice as often, and researchers were able to artificially recreate the effect in resistant tumors, significantly improving survival in mice, according to findings published in Nature Communications.
