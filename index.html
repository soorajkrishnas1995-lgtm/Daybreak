Biology · Aging Research

Immune cells losing their cleanup ability may be a key hidden driver of aging, study finds

Researchers found that tissue-resident macrophages, immune cells that live permanently within organs, become progressively less able with age to clear away damaged, inflammation-promoting cells, a decline that appears to drive aging throughout the body. Blocking a single receptor that binds an inflammation-linked hormone restored the cleanup process in aging mice, reducing chronic inflammation and preventing age-related frailty, excess fat accumulation and heart trouble, while also substantially slowing cognitive decline. The findings point to a specific, targetable mechanism behind multiple hallmarks of aging rather than treating each symptom separately.
