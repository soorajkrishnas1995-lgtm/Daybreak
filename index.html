Philadelphia · Cancer Research

Common sugar fructose helps chemotherapy-resistant ovarian cancer cells spread to new sites, study finds, pointing to new treatment targets

Researchers at the Wistar Institute found that ovarian cancer cells surviving chemotherapy release fructose as a chemical signal to neighboring tumor cells, suppressing their cholesterol production and weakening the molecular "glue" that normally keeps cells attached -- making it easier for cancer to detach and spread. The study, focused on high-grade serous ovarian cancer and published in Nature Aging, suggests sugary diets and cholesterol-lowering statins could influence how aggressively the cancer progresses, though researchers stressed patients should not stop taking prescribed statins since the effect hasn't yet been confirmed in clinical trials.

New laser technique 3D-prints ultra-hard tungsten carbide with less waste

Researchers at Hiroshima University and Mitsubishi Materials developed a hot-wire laser technique that softens rather than fully melts tungsten carbide-cobalt, letting manufacturers 3D-print the notoriously hard, expensive material only where needed -- producing defect-free, industrial-grade-hardness components while cutting the raw material waste that has long made the metal costly to shape.

Reanalyzed Spirit rover data adds evidence Mars was once covered by water

An Edith Cowan University researcher revisited more than 2,000 days of data from NASA's Spirit rover in Gusev Crater and found crystalline hematite and altered magnetite across 32 undisturbed soil sites -- minerals that typically form through interaction with water -- suggesting significant areas of Mars may once have been covered by it.
