Maryland · Astronomy

Astronomers find a dormant supermassive black hole 30,000 light-years from its galaxy's core

University of Maryland astronomers have identified a dormant supermassive black hole positioned far from the center of its host galaxy -- the first time scientists have found an inactive black hole at such a great distance from a galactic core. Because it wasn't actively feeding, the black hole would ordinarily have stayed invisible; its location was exposed only when it tore apart a passing star, producing a brief, powerful burst of light. The findings, published in The Astrophysical Journal Letters, suggest wandering black holes far from galactic centers may be more common than previously thought.
